# Photo-to-Flower Market Ephemera

Use a supplied photograph to make one 4:5 vertical page. Keep the upper 52–56% as the remembered real scene. Make the lower panel a restrained botanical ephemera arrangement on cream cotton paper: an original visual response to colour, flower shape, wrapping, season, and material—not a literal cut-out of the photo.

## Extract, then edit

Select only 3–5 visual cues from the source: flower form, stem/leaf gesture, wrapping material, market texture, and one colour relationship. If the photo has no flowers, translate the closest natural or tactile cues (leaves, fruit, ceramics, fabric, or light) without forcing florals.

## Lower-panel language

- Use one large botanical study and 2–5 supporting pieces: pressed-looking blooms, a blank vellum tag, torn wrapping paper, a small watercolour swatch, fine thread, or an abstract wax-seal-like circle.
- Keep shadows soft and real; let paper fibres and translucent vellum remain visible.
- Prefer blush, butter yellow, dusty lilac, faded coral, and muted green only when supported by the image. Avoid candy palettes and hard black outlines.
- Reserve 15–25% empty paper. The page should feel like a contemporary florist’s archive, not a wedding invitation or commercial mood board.

## Controls

- **Season:** spring, high summer, late summer, or winter greenhouse.
- **Material:** cotton paper, translucent vellum, matte wrapping, or recycled fibre.
- **Mood:** tender, sunlit, fresh, melancholic, or quietly celebratory.

## Guardrails

- Do not identify flowers with labels unless the user supplies verified names; use visual studies instead.
- Remove logos, price tags, signatures, copyrighted prints, and readable receipt text; rebuild only an original blank paper shape.
- Do not reproduce a florist’s proprietary packaging or an existing artist’s botanical plate.
- No watermarks, invented typography, decorative quote blocks, or cluttered sticker arrays.

Deliver one image and briefly name the selected visual cues.
